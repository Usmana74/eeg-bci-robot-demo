How this works

Controlling a robot with thought, in three steps
1 · A thought becomes a signal

When you imagine moving your left hand, right hand or feet, without moving, the motor part of your brain changes its electrical activity. Sensors on the scalp (EEG) record it. The wavy lines are a real recording.

2 · The decoder reads the signal

A trained decoder (a small AI model called EEGNet) looks at 4 seconds from 8 sensors and decides: left hand, right hand or feet. It runs right here in this page, in under a tenth of a second.

3 · The robot obeys

The robot copies the decision: left arm, right arm or a leg goes up. Green light means the decoder is right. Red light means it is wrong.

Please note, honestly: this is a replay of recorded brain data, not a live headset. The decoder never saw these two people during training; it learned from 8 other people. For Subject 1 it is right about 85% of the time; for Subject 2 about 42%. Random guessing is 33%. Mistakes are shown on purpose: this is how real brain-computer interfaces behave.
